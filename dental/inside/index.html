The room

Six machines, and what each one is for

None of it is there to be alarming. Take any part.

The overhead light

Aimed at your mouth, never at your eyes. If it catches you, say so — it moves.

The delivery unit

Every instrument hangs within the dentist's reach, so nobody walks away mid-treatment.

The chair

Reclines slowly, and stops wherever you are comfortable. You can ask it to sit you up at any point.

Suction and water

The two hoses that make the noise. Water rinses, suction clears — neither touches a nerve.

The stool

Rolls, so the dentist moves instead of leaning over you.

The foot pedal

Controls the drill and the chair by foot, which is why gloved hands never leave your mouth to reach for a switch.

The tray

Eight instruments. Three of them see you.

A full tray is set out because it has to be sterile before anyone knows what is needed. Most of it goes back untouched.

Thirty minutes

What happens, and when

A first visit runs about half an hour. Nothing is done to your teeth without telling you the cost first.

0–5 min

You sit down

We ask what brought you in and whether anything hurts. Nothing goes near your mouth yet.

5–15 min

We look

Mirror and probe, tooth by tooth, and the gum line. You will hear numbers called out — they are gum measurements, not a score.

15–20 min

X-ray, only if needed

One image, a few seconds, and you see it on the screen at the same moment we do.

20–30 min

The plan and the price

What needs doing, what can wait, what each part costs. In writing, before you agree to anything.
